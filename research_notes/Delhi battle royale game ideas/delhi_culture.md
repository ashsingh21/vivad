## What everyday Delhi and NCR slang is authentic, with meaning and when it is used?

### Takeaway
The words named in the brief (bhai, bawaal, panga, scene, jugaad) are real and in everyday use, and two Tribune columns give the funniest ready-made Haryanvi lines. I found no authoritative Delhi slang dictionary, though. Most "Delhi slang" lists online are really pan-Hindi, so a Delhi native must check the final list.

**Schoolyard codes with hand signs (recalled in 2020, so possibly dated)**

- **katti ("cutty")** means "we are fighting." The sign is a thumbnail hooked behind the front teeth and flicked forward. **abba** is the make-up call, with the thumb in the mouth and fingers wriggling like playing a trumpet — [Madhavan, 2020](https://mrm.substack.com/p/the-internet-personified-a-small)

**Cop nicknames**

- In Delhi a cop is a "mama" or "thulla." "Pandu" is the Mumbai word — [The Quint](https://thequint.com/neon/fun/ten-words-you-need-to-know-if-you-want-to-talk-like-a-gully-boy) (snippet)
- "Thulla" has a real legal history. A constable filed a criminal defamation complaint over the word, and in 2016 the Delhi High Court asked Arvind Kejriwal to explain it because it is not in the dictionary — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/130716/delhi-hc-asks-arvind-kejriwal-to-explain-meaning-of-thulla-remark.html) (snippet); [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/thulla-remarks-hc-allows-kejriwal-to-withdraw-plea-challenging-summon-118080600994_1.html) (snippet)
- Sources disagree on its reach. One calls it an eastern India word for a lazy cop with a big belly — [OneIndia](https://www.oneindia.com/india/kejriwal-s-remark-what-really-is-the-meaning-thulla-2152442.html) (snippet); contradicted by [The Quint](https://thequint.com/amp/story/news/india/if-you-dont-mind-cops-whats-the-problem-with-thulla) (snippet), which treats it as a long-running nickname alongside pandu and mama.

**Old Delhi: Karkhandari**

- This was the speech of artisans, traders and factory workers. Narang's 1961 estimate was about 50,000 speakers between Chandni Chowk, Faiz Bazar, Asaf Ali Road and Lahori Gate — [Nair, 2017](https://www.dawn.com/news/1352112)
- Its core area was near Jama Masjid and Lal Kuan — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karkhandari_Urdu) (snippet)
- Words are clipped or reshaped: itne mein becomes itte mein, neeche becomes neechu, vahan becomes vaan, usne becomes visne, kabhi becomes kabhun, achanak becomes achanchak — [Nair, 2017](https://www.dawn.com/news/1352112)
- It has a singsong, friendly cadence. Outsiders often called it coarse and funny. Very few speakers remain — [Nair, 2017](https://www.dawn.com/news/1352112)

### Inferences

- Delhi is not one voice. Four layers would make bots sound like different people: college and office Hinglish (CP), Punjabi-tinged Hindi (South and West Delhi), Haryanvi (Gurugram and the outer villages), and Karkhandari-tinged Old Delhi speech. Tie the voice to a character's job or area, never to a caste or faith.
- The Haryanvi lines are the strongest material here. They are funny because they are rude without any swearing, which is what a teen-rated game needs.
- The HUD already uses Hinglish (Jaan, Kavach, Goli, "Safe zone mein ho" in `crates/game/src/hud.rs`), so barks are a natural extension, not a new tone.
- The katti and abba hand signs and the chop gesture would make very local emotes. Abba could be the squad truce or revive emote.
- Do not lift film dialogue. Borrow the rhythm and write original lines.

### Gaps

- No authoritative Delhi slang source found. The best Delhi-specific material is one personal lexicon and a 2011 column. The Quint's Delhi list cut off before its word list, so its 12 words are unknown.
- "Tau" and "Chaudhary": I found no source defining them. From general knowledge, tau is a respectful word for an older man, and Chaudhary is a title used by landowning groups such as Jats and Gujjars. If that is right, a "Chaudhary" character is caste-coded, so a generic "Tau" is safer. This needs checking.
- Common words I could not source, so they need a native check: paaji, bhaisaab, oye, abey, tashan, faltu, bakwas, ullu, gadha, sahi hai. "Jhand" may have a vulgar root, so check it before use.
- Jhakaas, bindaas and vaat lag gayi: I recall these as Mumbai words, but could not confirm it. Jhakaas was heard in Gurgaon in [2011](https://thedelhiwalla.blog/2011/11/09/city-culture-delhis-emerging-lingo/). Native check needed.
- "Lag gaye" looks safe, but I recall the form "uski lag gayi" can sound vulgar. Avoid that form until a native confirms.
- Delhi Crime, Paatal Lok and other web series were not researched. No language study of "Delhi Hindi" was found. The film notes are reviewers' impressions.

## Which words are clean enough for a teen-rated game, and which abusive words must be avoided?

### Takeaway
Real Delhi street abuse is mostly sex-based, so authentic swearing is not usable: the flavour has to come from clean comic put-downs. The biggest traps are everyday words with caste roots such as chhapri and kameena, and any stereotype joke, because PEGI treats discrimination as 18-only content.

### Cited Findings

**Rating rules**

- PEGI 12 allows only mild bad language. PEGI 16 can go further — [PEGI](https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean)
- Discrimination appears only at PEGI 18 and is always restricted to it. It covers ethnic, religious, nationalistic or other stereotypes likely to encourage hatred — [PEGI](https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean)
- PEGI 12 allows non-realistic violence toward human-like characters. PEGI 16 covers violence that looks as it would in real life — [PEGI](https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean)
- India has no dedicated law on game content — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/gaming/914002/video-game-censorship-indian-purview) (snippet)

**Sex-based abuse (RED)**

- Delhi's most common swear word is a sexual body word, used for friends and enemies alike — [Madhavan, 2020](https://mrm.substack.com/p/the-internet-personified-a-small)
- Ch\*tiya is a cuss word "everyone" uses. KLPD, the joke form chutium sulphate and the film pun DK Bose are all vulgar — [The Delhi Walla](https://thedelhiwalla.blog/2011/11/09/city-culture-delhis-emerging-lingo/)
- A real Delhi wrong-parking quarrel made headlines for sister-based abuse — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/behen-ke-le-mother-daughter-duo-gets-into-ugly-quarrel-with-man-objecting-to-wrong-parking-in-delhi-video-viral)
- In Haryana, Sunil Jaglan's Gaali Band Ghar campaign targets sexist slurs, with families logging gaalis on a wall chart — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaali_Band_Ghar) (snippet); [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/changemakers/sunil-jaglan-gaali-band-campaign-haryana-verbal-abuse-language-change-india-10650254) (snippet)

**Caste words**

- Listed as casteist insults: bhangi, chamar, chandaal, dhobi, kanjar, kameena, bhand, mahar, malech, pariah. Also named as words for lowliness: junglee, kasaai, naai, adivasi, kathodi, dedhgujari — [Lochan, Homegrown, 2021](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/casteism-in-our-words-10-casteist-slurs-and-why-we-need-to-stop-throwing-them-around)
- Kameena now just means scoundrel, but in Persian it meant low or low-caste — [Lochan, Homegrown, 2021](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/casteism-in-our-words-10-casteist-slurs-and-why-we-need-to-stop-throwing-them-around)
- Using chamar as an insult may be an offence under the SC/ST Act (Supreme Court, 2008). The Court held in 2017 that calling someone dhobi or Harijan is offensive — [Lochan, Homegrown, 2021](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/casteism-in-our-words-10-casteist-slurs-and-why-we-need-to-stop-throwing-them-around)
- **Chhapri** is the main trap, because it is used heavily online as a casual put-down — [Homegrown](https://homegrown.co.in/amp/story/homegrown-voices/heres-why-using-the-word-chapri-isnt-as-harmless-as-you-think-it-might-be) (snippet). Academic commentary ties it to the Chapparband caste — [FLAME University](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/digital-exclusion-how-chapri-and-digital-aesthetics-reinforce-caste-hierarchies) (snippet)
- Its caste link is less widely known than for other slurs — [Doing Sociology](https://doingsociology.org/2024/06/12/chapri-fashion-a-disruption-of-cultural-domination-in-the-field-of-mens-fashion-in-india-sohom-roy/) (snippet). So the exact origin is contested, but the casteist reading is public: Swara Bhasker called it out in 2025 — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/bollywood/swara-bhasker-hits-back-at-troll-who-insulted-husband-fahad-ahmad-with-casteist-slur-1503468627.html) (snippet)

### Inferences

**Working rules**

1. Mock ego, never identity. Parking pride, VIP swagger and clinginess are fair targets. Caste, faith, birthplace, gender and body are not.
2. A word fails if it needs a woman's body, a community's name or a place of birth to be funny.
3. Build character types on behaviour (parking uncle, chep, VIP), never on a community. No Bihari labourer joke, no Nepali guard joke, no Northeast momo seller joke, no Sardar joke.
4. Keep momos and chowmein as food on a stall sign. Never shout them at a character.
5. "Bhaiya" stays only as a polite way to address a vendor or driver, never as a label.

## What do real Delhi street fights and arguments look like, and how can they be shown in a funny, non-glorifying way?

### Takeaway
Real Delhi fights start over tiny things, such as a blocked gate, a scratch or "my spot," then escalate through pride, phone calls for backup and sometimes guns, and people have died. The comedy can live in the pettiness and the crowd, but it must stop well before the violence and never recreate a real case.

### Cited Findings

**How parking fights start and grow** (all from [ThePrint ground report](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/delhi-parking-murder-court-cases-road-rage/2911224/))

- Triggers include a car blocking a gate, someone taking a spot another person thinks of as theirs, scratched bumpers, and disputes over who owns a stretch of road.
- Older buildings often have one parking spot per floor, but one family may own four or five cars. Streets once used by children to play now store cars.
- Escalation in the Preet Vihar case ran through repeated arguments, a rejected offer of another spot, a phone call to a brother, a challenge to fight in person, reported alcohol, then shots fired.
- Neighbours often try to calm things down. Many others heard the noise and stayed in because such nights had become routine.
- RWAs, relatives and community WhatsApp groups get pulled in.
- Delhi Police control room calls about parking violence rose from 2,413 in 2021 to over 7,000 in 2025.
- Delhi has over 80 lakh cars. A Dwarka lawyer estimates six in ten of his cases are parking-related, and he puts it down to people wanting power over others.

**Real harm**

- A Preet Vihar parking argument ended in a fatal shooting in April 2026 — [Brut](https://www.brut.media/in/articles/india/society/from-argument-to-gunshot-parking-dispute-in-preet-vihar-turned-violent) (snippet)

### Inferences

**The funny version of the ladder.** Keep the first steps, cut the last.

1. Petty trigger: blocked gate, scratch, "meri jagah."
2. Rising pride: loud talk, big threats, nothing happens.
3. Backup: phone calls and "Bhai ko bula."
4. Crowd: ring of people, phones up, one uncle offering advice.
5. Stop here. Replace weapons with a comic exit, such as a mother phoning, or the Aandhi arriving and everyone running.

**Rules for funny but not glorifying**

- The joke is how small the cause is, never the hurt that follows.
- Ambient NPC quarrels never have weapons and never end in injury.
- The swagger always loses. A "Do you know who I am?" bark should play when downed, so the ego looks silly.
- No real cases, no real victims, no gangster names, and no quarrel set beside a real place of worship.
- No violence against women played for laughs. No collar-grab or slap animations aimed at drivers, who are often poor migrants.
- Do not reuse the real threat lines. They are too dark.
